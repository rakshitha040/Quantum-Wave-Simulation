# Quantum Hamiltonian Simulation of the 1D Wave Equation

This module implements a **quantum Hamiltonian approach** for solving the one-dimensional wave equation using **Qiskit statevector simulation** and **Suzuki-Trotter time evolution**.

It forms the quantum part of the larger research project:

**Classical vs. Quantum: A Comparative Study of Finite Difference and Hamiltonian Simulation Approaches to the 1D Wave Equation**

---

## 1. Governing Equation

The one-dimensional wave equation is:

**∂²u/∂t² = c² ∂²u/∂x²**

where:

- **u(x,t)** is the wave displacement
- **c** is the wave propagation speed
- **x** is the spatial coordinate
- **t** is time

The spatial domain is discretized before constructing the Hamiltonian representation.

---

## 2. Hamiltonian Formulation

The second-order wave equation is reformulated as a first-order system.

The resulting system is represented using a block Hamiltonian of the form:

**H = (c/Δx) [ 0    B  ]  
              [ Bᵀ   0  ]**

where:

- **Δx** is the spatial grid spacing
- **B** is the matrix obtained from the spatial discretization
- **Bᵀ** is its transpose
- **H** is the Hamiltonian used for quantum time evolution

The corresponding quantum evolution is governed by:

**|ψ(t)⟩ = e⁻ⁱᴴᵗ |ψ(0)⟩**

Thus, instead of directly solving the original second-order differential equation using a classical time-stepping scheme, the problem is expressed as Hamiltonian time evolution.

---

## 3. Suzuki-Trotter Time Evolution

The Hamiltonian is decomposed into simpler components:

**H = Σⱼ Hⱼ**

The exact evolution operator:

**e⁻ⁱᴴᵗ**

is approximated using Suzuki-Trotter decomposition.

For first-order Trotterization:

**e⁻ⁱᴴᵗ ≈ (∏ⱼ e⁻ⁱᴴⱼΔt)ʳ**

where:

- **Δt** is the evolution time step
- **r** is the number of Trotter repetitions
- **Hⱼ** are the decomposed Hamiltonian terms

Increasing the number of Trotter repetitions provides a way to investigate the convergence of the approximate quantum evolution toward the exact Hamiltonian evolution.

---

## 4. Quantum Simulation

The implementation uses **Qiskit** and **Qiskit Aer** for quantum statevector simulation.

The workflow is:

1D Wave Equation
       ↓
Spatial Discretization
       ↓
First-Order System
       ↓
Hamiltonian Construction
       ↓
Hamiltonian Decomposition
       ↓
Suzuki-Trotter Evolution
       ↓
Quantum Statevector Simulation
       ↓
Wave Solution / Results

The quantum evolution is performed using the Hamiltonian formulation rather than directly implementing the classical finite-difference time-stepping scheme as a quantum circuit.

## 8. Trotter Convergence

The number of Trotter repetitions **r** controls the approximation of the Hamiltonian time-evolution operator.

For smaller values of **r**, the Trotter approximation can introduce a larger time-evolution error.

The convergence study therefore evaluates the quantum solution for increasing values of **r** and examines the resulting behavior.

The Trotterization study is used to understand the relationship between:

- Trotter step count
- Approximation error
- Quantum time-evolution accuracy

## 10. Methodology

The quantum implementation follows these main stages:

### Step 1 — Spatial Discretization

The one-dimensional computational domain is discretized into spatial grid points with spacing **Δx**.

### Step 2 — First-Order Reformulation

The second-order wave equation is converted into a first-order system suitable for Hamiltonian representation.

### Step 3 — Hamiltonian Construction

A block Hamiltonian is constructed from the spatial discretization matrices.

### Step 4 — Hamiltonian Decomposition

The Hamiltonian is decomposed into terms that can be used for Trotterized time evolution.

### Step 5 — Suzuki-Trotter Evolution

The quantum state is evolved using first-order Suzuki-Trotter decomposition.

### Step 6 — Statevector Simulation

Qiskit Aer is used to simulate the resulting quantum evolution.

### Step 7 — Analysis

The resulting states and numerical quantities are processed to study the behavior and convergence of the quantum solution.

## 11. Relation to the Classical Solver

The classical module of the project uses the finite difference method with leapfrog time integration.

This quantum module follows a different formulation:

| Classical Approach | Quantum Approach |
|---|---|
| Finite Difference Method | Hamiltonian formulation |
| Leapfrog time integration | Quantum time evolution |
| Classical numerical state | Quantum statevector |
| Direct time stepping | Suzuki-Trotter evolution |
| Classical numerical computation | Qiskit statevector simulation |

The two approaches are developed independently so that their formulations, accuracy, and computational characteristics can be studied comparatively.

---

## 12. Research Objective

The purpose of this module is not simply to reproduce the classical solver using a quantum framework.

The objective is to investigate the **Hamiltonian formulation of the wave equation and its quantum time evolution**, and to study the numerical behavior introduced by Suzuki-Trotter approximation.

The results from this module will contribute to the broader classical-versus-quantum analysis of the one-dimensional wave equation.
